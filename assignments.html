For A1, A2 and A3

Basics for every PDF

These apply to A1, A2 and A3. Use any layout tool you like (Canva, InDesign, Keynote, PowerPoint, Google Slides) and export one PDF.

Landscape 16:9 pagesSet the page to 16:9 before you start, for example 1920 x 1080 px or 33.87 x 19.05 cm (Canva "Presentation 16:9"). Every page in the PDF is the same size.
Export to PDFExport your layout as PDF. Don't upload a Word file, a Canva link or loose images. A1 is best as one PDF (see A1 for the 4-file option).
Let the photos leadShow photos large, on a plain background. Don't put text over your photos, don't add drop shadows, frames or stickers.
Readable textOne or two typefaces at most. Body text at least 18 pt on a 16:9 page, so it reads without zooming. Left-align paragraphs.
Caption every photoA short line under each photo: number, title and year. The number matches your file name. No camera settings needed. See the caption examples below.
Page numbers and proofreadingNumber your pages. The briefs ask for writing free of grammar and spelling errors, so read it aloud once before you export.
Your own photos, this semesterEvery photo is taken by you this semester for this course. Reference any photographer or artist who inspired you.
AI on images: Beyond the Lens onlyAI collaboration on images is accepted only in an A1 journal using a Week 3 technique (Beyond the Lens), declared and referenced. Everywhere else, use AI only to plan or check grammar, never to generate, edit or alter images.

Caption format

Number · title · year. Keep it on one line. Camera settings are not needed.

1.3 Steam and Hands, 2026 IMG_4021.JPG my favourite photo!!

In A1, 1.3 means Journal 1, photo 3. In A2, use the image number in your sequence: 7.

Exporting your JPGs

A1 and A2 also need a zip of high-res JPGs (300 dpi, full size). In Lightroom Classic, File > Export:

  • File Settings: JPEG, Quality 90 to 100, Color Space sRGB
  • Image Sizing: untick "Resize to Fit", Resolution 300 pixels per inch
  • File Naming: rename to the course pattern (see Files)
  • Output Sharpening: Screen, Standard

The theme

rễ-root: connect your personal identity to a visual story

All four journals explore one idea about you: where you come from, what you carry, who you are becoming. Identity is also the most common theme in real open calls, so this is practice for work beyond the classroom.

"The most personal is the most creative."Martin Scorsese, quoted by Bong Joon-ho

1Step 1Know your techniques

The techniques are not styles to copy, but methods you apply to support your concept. Choose the ones that strengthen meaning, intention and visual communication. Each of your 4 journals uses a technique from a different week. You can combine techniques inside one journal. The same board is on the AS1 Miro board, which has the latest format notes.

2Step 2Plan your 4 journals

Answer the questions below. Weeks you have already used are locked in the other journals, so you can't pick two from the same week. Your plan stays in this browser only. Copy it to bring to your lecturer consultation.

3Step 3Lay out your PDF, page by page

Every journal follows the same flow: overview, the 4 photos one by one, reflection, mood board, behind the scenes.

What to write in each reflection

Focus on how and why you chose the technique(s) and the subject. Not a diary of what happened in class. Suggested headings:

  1. Why this technique. How does it support your rễ-root idea? What does it do that a straight photo can't?
  2. Why this subject. What part of your identity or story does it hold?
  3. How you made it. Where, when, materials, light or editing steps, and what you changed between attempts.
  4. What you learned. What worked, what didn't, what you would try next. Point to your mood board and reference the artists.

Two ways to place it: Type 1, a short note beside each photo; Type 2, one text after the 4 photos. Either way, 250 to 500 words per journal in total.

If a Week 3 journal uses AI collaboration, say which tool, on which photos and what it did.

One PDF, or four

One PDF with all 4 journals and all 16 photos is preferred: one upload, and your journal reads start to finish.

If one file is too big to upload, you may submit 4 PDFs, one per journal:

A1-1_peterparker_s1234567.pdf A1-2_peterparker_s1234567.pdf A1-3_peterparker_s1234567.pdf A1-4_peterparker_s1234567.pdf

Each of the 4 files needs its own cover, the OneDrive link and the Statement of Authorship, so no file is incomplete on its own.

Laying out the images

  • Same margins and the same position for every image page, so the photos change and nothing else does.
  • Don't crop a portrait image to fill a landscape page. Leave space on both sides.
  • Pairs are fine when two images work together, but keep most images on their own page.
  • Your JPG numbers, PDF order and Week 12 showcase order are the same sequence.
  • Banned topic: a set of only abstract images (for example close-ups of walls). Confirm your topic with your lecturer.

Writing a reflection, not a diary

  • Explain decisions, not just events: "I moved the shoot indoors because..." rather than "Then I went to District 4."
  • Refer to your figures in the text: "(Fig. 4)".
  • Be specific about challenges. "The light was hard" says little; "midday light flattened the faces, so I moved the shoot to 17:00" says a lot.
  • Evaluate honestly. Saying what didn't work, and why, is part of the mark.

Before you upload

Files, naming and OneDrive

File names tell us the order of your images, so they matter. Use lowercase, no spaces, and your own name and student number.

What to upload on Canvas

Your OneDrive folder

A1_peterparker_s1234567/ RAW/ journal1/ .CR3 .NEF .ARW ... journal2/ journal3/ journal4/ behind-the-scenes/
  1. Upload the original RAW files, not edited exports.
  2. Share the top folder: Share > set the link so people in RMIT with the link can view > Copy link.
  3. Paste the link into your PDF and make it clickable.
  4. Test it: open the link in a private browser window and sign in with a different RMIT account if you can.
